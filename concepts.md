<div class="idea-container">
  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_incentive#The_original_cobra_effect" target="_blank">The Cobra Effect</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">When a solution to a problem makes the problem worse due to unintended consequences</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: British offered bounties for dead cobras, so people bred cobras to collect the reward.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streisand_effect" target="_blank">The Streisand Effect</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">When an attempt to hide, remove, or censor information has the unintended consequence of further publicizing that information</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Barbra Streisand sued to remove photos of her home from the internet, causing millions to view them.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox_of_tolerance" target="_blank">The Paradox of Tolerance</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">If a society is tolerant without limit, its ability to be tolerant will eventually be destroyed by the intolerant</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: An online platform with no moderation eventually becomes dominated by its most extreme users.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paradox_of_Choice" target="_blank">The Paradox of Choice</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">Too many options can lead to anxiety, decision paralysis, and dissatisfaction</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Spending 30 minutes browsing Netflix, then giving up without watching anything.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivorship_bias" target="_blank">Survivorship Bias</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">Focusing on successes while ignoring failures that didn't survive to be examined</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Studying successful startups while ignoring thousands that failed using the same strategies.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law" target="_blank">Goodhart's Law</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: When schools are judged by test scores, teachers "teach to the test" rather than educate.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning%E2%80%93Kruger_effect" target="_blank">The Dunning-Kruger Effect</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">A cognitive bias in which people with low ability at a task overestimate their ability</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Someone who took a few guitar lessons thinks they're ready to perform at a concert.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunk_cost#Fallacy_effect" target="_blank">The Sunk Cost Fallacy</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">Continuing a course of action because of past investment rather than future value</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Finishing a bad movie because you already paid for the ticket.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson%27s_law" target="_blank">Parkinson's Law</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">Work expands to fill the time available for its completion</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: A report given a week deadline takes a week; the same report given two days takes two days.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_bias" target="_blank">Confirmation Bias</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">The tendency to favor information that confirms existing beliefs while ignoring contradictory evidence</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: Only reading news sources that align with your political views.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="idea-card">
    <div class="idea-title">
      <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impostor_syndrome" target="_blank">Imposter Syndrome</a>
    </div>
    <p class="idea-description">A psychological pattern in which one doubts one's accomplishments and has a persistent internalized fear of being exposed as a "fraud"</p>
    <p class="idea-example">Example: A published author feels their book's success was a fluke and dreads being "found out."</p>
  </div>
